# Stochastic

Stochastic describes systems, processes, or models governed by random probability distributions rather than a deterministic, fixed plan. It refers to phenomena that happen by chance, often changing over time in unpredictable ways. It is the opposite of deterministic (predictable) and is widely used in physics, biology, and finance. [1, 2, 3, 4]  
Key Aspects of Stochastic Processes & Modeling 

• Definition: A stochastic process is a collection of random variables (often indexed by time) that model the evolution of a system, such as Brownian motion. 
• Types of Models: Key stochastic models include Markov chains (discrete, state-to-state), random walks, and continuous processes like Gaussian noise or geometric Brownian motion. 
• Stochastic Matrix: A matrix where rows or columns sum to 1, used to determine probabilities of transitioning from one state to another (e.g., Markov chains). [5, 6, 7, 8, 9]  

Applications 

• Physics: Used to model random, unpredictable events such as radioactive decay or molecular diffusion (Brownian motion). 
• Biology: Describes stochastic gene expression or environmental fluctuations impacting population dynamics. 
• Finance: Stochastic calculus is used to model the fluctuating prices of assets, such as option pricing. [3, 6, 9, 10, 11]  

Opposite: Deterministic—A system with no randomness where the same input always produces the same output. [12, 13]
